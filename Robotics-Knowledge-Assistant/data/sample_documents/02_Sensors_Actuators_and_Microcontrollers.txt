2.1 Proprioceptive and Exteroceptive Sensors in Robotics
Accurate state estimation depends on matching sensor modality to physical dynamics:
- Quadrature Optical/Magnetic Encoders: Output two 90-degree phase-shifted pulse channels (A and B) plus an optional Index (Z) channel. Decoding rising and falling edges in 4x quadrature mode multiplies resolution by 4 and determines rotation direction.
- Inertial Measurement Units (IMU): Combine a 3-axis MEMS accelerometer (linear acceleration + gravity vector) and a 3-axis gyroscope (angular velocity). 9-DOF IMUs add a magnetometer. Sensor fusion algorithms such as Madgwick or Extended Kalman Filters (EKF) eliminate gyroscope drift and accelerometer high-frequency noise.
- Ultrasonic Sensors (HC-SR04): Measure time-of-flight of 40 kHz acoustic pulses (distance = speed_of_sound * duration / 2). Effective range is 2 cm to 400 cm, though oblique specular surfaces cause false echoes.
- 2D/3D LiDAR: Uses pulsed laser time-of-flight or phase-shift measurement to construct dense point clouds for SLAM and obstacle avoidance.

2.2 Microcontrollers: Arduino vs. ESP32 Architecture
Embedded robotics controllers bridge sensors and motor drivers with deterministic timing:
- Arduino Uno / Mega (ATmega328P / ATmega2560): 8-bit AVR architecture running at 16 MHz, 5V logic levels, hardware PWM timers, UART/SPI/I2C peripherals. Ideal for introductory lab rigs, simple servo sequencing, and 5V industrial sensor interfacing, though limited in RAM (2 KB on Uno) and floating-point throughput.
- ESP32 (Xtensa LX6 Dual-Core / RISC-V): 32-bit dual-core MCU running up to 240 MHz with 520 KB SRAM, integrated Wi-Fi (802.11 b/g/n) and Bluetooth/BLE, 12-bit ADCs, hardware MCPWM motor-control peripherals, PCNT pulse counters for hardware quadrature encoding, and FreeRTOS multitasking support. Operates at 3.3V logic, requiring level shifters when interfaced with 5V peripherals.

2.3 Communication Buses and PWM Signal Conditioning
Robotics subsystems exchange sensor telemetry and actuator commands over standard serial protocols:
- I2C (Inter-Integrated Circuit): Two-wire synchronous bus (SDA, SCL) with 7-bit addressing and pull-up resistors (typically 4.7 kOhm). Suited for short-range board-level IMUs and OLEDs at 100 kHz–400 kHz.
- SPI (Serial Peripheral Interface): Four-wire full-duplex bus (MOSI, MISO, SCLK, CS) supporting >10 MHz throughput for high-rate encoders, SD storage, and camera modules.
- UART / RS-485 / CAN Bus: Asynchronous serial and differential buses. CAN bus provides deterministic arbitration, CRC error checking, and EMI immunity across long robot wiring harnesses.
- Pulse Width Modulation (PWM): Controls DC motor average voltage via duty cycle D = t_on / T_period (typically 10–25 kHz for silent motor driving) or encodes hobby servo position (50 Hz, 1000–2000 microsecond pulse width).
